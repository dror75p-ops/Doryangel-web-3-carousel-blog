What's Your Bronx Tenant's August Move-Out Actually Costing You in Lost Rent and Turnover?

An August tenant turnover in the Bronx quietly drains $2,500–$4,500 per unit before you re-rent — and most owners never see the full bill until it's too late.

The Real August Move-Out Bill: $2,500–$4,500 Per Unit, Plus 15–30 Days of Lost Rent

If you're staring down an August lease-end in the Bronx and feeling that familiar knot in your stomach, you're not imagining the cost. A typical Bronx turnover runs $2,500–$4,500 per unit in hard costs, plus 15–30 days of vacancy at median Bronx rents of $1,500–$2,000/month. That's easily 1.5 to 3 months of rent gone before your next tenant hands you a check — and August is the worst month to absorb it because roughly a quarter of NYC's annual moves happen right now.

We've walked hundreds of Bronx owners through this exact squeeze, and the pattern is almost always the same: the visible costs (paint, cleaning, listing) get budgeted, and the invisible ones (compliance fines, deposit penalties, missed rent increases) quietly eat the rest.

Where the Money Actually Goes

Let's break down what a real August turnover costs on a typical Grand Concourse or Fordham two-bedroom renting at $1,900/month:

  • Lost rent (21 days average): ~$1,330
  • Interior repainting (HPD §27-2005 required every 3 years): $800–$1,500
  • Deep cleaning: $250–$450
  • Minor repairs (patching, fixtures, appliance touch-ups): $400–$900
  • Listing, showings, screening time: $200–$500
  • Locksmith / rekey: $150–$250

That's $3,130–$4,930 before you factor in any surprise HPD violations or deposit disputes. In our experience managing Bronx multifamilies, owners who self-manage almost always underestimate this by 30–40% because they forget to charge their own time.

The Compliance Traps That Turn a Turnover Into a Lawsuit

August move-outs are legally loaded in NYC. Three specific traps catch Bronx owners every year:

What Happens If You Return the Security Deposit Late?

Under NYC Housing Maintenance Code §27-2004, you have 14 days from lease termination to return the deposit with an itemized deduction statement. Miss that window and you owe $5–$10 per day in statutory damages, plus 9% annual interest, and you forfeit the right to keep any portion for damages. We've seen Bronx owners lose $2,400 in deposit deductions because their handyman took three weeks to itemize the repairs. The fix is boring but effective: document damage the day of walk-through with dated photos, and issue the itemization within 10 days — not 14.

Are You Actually Compliant With the 3-Year Repaint Rule?

HPD §27-2005 requires interior repainting every three years. If the outgoing tenant lived there 3+ years and you skip it, the incoming tenant can file an HPD complaint within weeks. Each violation runs $250–$2,500, and HPD can issue a mandatory repair order that puts your unit under inspection scrutiny for months. Painting during vacancy is dramatically cheaper than painting around a new tenant and paying a Class B violation.

Did You Document Good Cause for Non-Renewal?

New York's Good Cause Eviction Law (effective 2024, still governing 2026 turnovers) requires landlords in covered buildings to document a legitimate reason for non-renewal or a rent increase above the threshold. If your August move-out is actually a non-renewal you initiated, sloppy notice can trigger class action exposure and attorney's fees. Bronx housing court is currently running 21–30 days on contested turnover cases under CPLR §213 — and that's 21–30 more days of zero rent.

The Hidden Cost Nobody Talks About: Frozen Rent Growth

Here's the one that stings quietly. DHCR guidelines allowed 3%–4.5% rent increases on 1–2 year renewals in the last cycle. During vacancy, that increase opportunity is frozen — you can reset market rent on the new lease, sure, but every day the unit sits empty is a day you're earning $0 instead of the higher renewal rate you could have negotiated with the outgoing tenant. On a $1,900 unit, a 4% bump you didn't capture because you were focused on turnover chaos is $912 of missed annual income. Multiply that across a 6-unit Bronx building and you're at $5,400/year in silent leakage.

How to Cut a Bronx August Turnover Down to 10–14 Days

The owners we work with who consistently hit sub-two-week turnovers do five specific things:

  1. Start showings 45 days before move-out. New York law lets you show occupied units with reasonable notice. Don't wait until the keys are back.
  2. Pre-schedule the painter and cleaner for the day after move-out — not the week after. August contractor availability disappears fast.
  3. Do a pre-move-out walkthrough at day 20. This surfaces damage while the tenant can still fix it (or agree to the deduction in writing), which saves you deposit-dispute drama.
  4. Screen aggressively but legally. Use written criteria applied identically to every applicant to stay clear of NYC Human Rights Law claims — one discrimination complaint costs more than a year of vacancy.
  5. Track your compliance deadlines in one place. The 14-day deposit clock, the 3-year paint cycle, and Good Cause notice windows all run on different calendars. DoryAngel's free Compliance Calendar flags each of these automatically so nothing slips through during the August rush.

The Bottom Line for Bronx Owners

A Bronx August turnover isn't a $500 inconvenience — it's a $3,000–$5,000 event when you count vacancy, compliance risk, and frozen rent growth. The owners who protect their margins treat move-out as a 60-day project starting the moment notice is given, not a scramble that begins when the keys hit the counter.

If this August is already looking messy, the good news is that most of the damage is preventable with the right checklist and a tight timeline. You don't have to eat three months of rent to turn a unit — you just need the process pinned down before the moving truck shows up.
