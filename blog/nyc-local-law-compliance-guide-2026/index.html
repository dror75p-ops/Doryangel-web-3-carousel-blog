Are You About to Miss a 2026 Bronx Compliance Deadline? The Fines That Hit Hardest

The Bronx leads all five boroughs in building violations, with fines running $250–$1,000 a day. Here are the NYC local laws and filing deadlines every Bronx landlord must track in 2026 — and what missing each one costs you.

If you own property in the Bronx, 2026 is not the year to look away from your compliance calendar. New York City is aggressively enforcing a wave of local laws — and the Bronx consistently leads all five boroughs in open building violations. Fines are running $250 to $1,000 per day for unresolved issues, and a single missed filing can snowball into thousands before you ever see a warning.

Here is what belongs on every Bronx owner's radar right now, and what missing each one actually costs you.

Local Law 84 & 97 — Energy Benchmarking and Emissions Caps

Who it affects: Buildings over 25,000 sq ft (or two or more on the same lot totaling over 50,000 sq ft).

Local Law 84 requires you to submit annual energy and water usage to the city by May 1st each year through the EPA's ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager. Fines accrue quarterly with no cap — non-compliant owners have racked up cumulative fines over $10,000.

Local Law 97 layers on greenhouse-gas emissions caps, live since January 2024, with penalties of $268 per metric ton of CO2 over your limit. Limits tighten again in 2030, so even smaller buildings should benchmark now to plan capital work.

Local Law 11 (FISP) — Façade Inspections

Buildings six stories or taller must have their façades inspected every five years by a qualified exterior wall inspector. Cycle 9 filings are ongoing, and the DOB has sharply raised penalties for late or missing reports — up to $1,000 per month until you file. On a Bronx walk-up that's an easy five-figure hit for simply forgetting a deadline.

Local Law 152 — Gas Line Safety Inspections

Who it affects: Any building with three or more residential units, or commercial space, that has gas service.

A Licensed Master Plumber must inspect all gas piping and file a report, on a cycle set by your community district. The plumber's certification (GPS1) is submitted within 60 days, with a GPS2 follow-up within 120 days if repairs are needed. Bronx Community Districts 4, 6, 8, 9, and 16 have inspection cycles closing by the end of 2026. Miss yours and you face immediate violations — and potential shut-off orders if an unsafe condition surfaces late.

Local Law 31 — Lead Paint Testing

Who it affects: Buildings built before 1960 with a child under age 6 living in a unit.

You must complete XRF testing or paint-chip sampling in all applicable units and common areas and file results with HPD; records must be kept for 10 years. The phased deadlines have already passed, so if you haven't tested, you are accumulating violations right now — and HPD is actively pursuing non-compliant Bronx landlords.

Know Your Violation Classes

NYC sorts housing violations into three tiers, and the fine math is brutal at the top:

Class Example Fix Deadline Fine Range
A (non-hazardous) Peeling paint in a common area 90 days $50–$150
B (hazardous) Broken heating system 30 days $50–$500
C (immediately hazardous) No heat in winter, lead paint, rodents 24 hours $250–$1,000/day

The Bronx has the highest volume of open Class C violations in the city. Worse, HPD's Alternative Enforcement Program targets the 250 most-distressed buildings — many of them in the Bronx — where the city can make emergency repairs itself and bill you directly, with fees on top.

The Annual Filings You Can't Skip

Even in a year with no new law, these recur — put them on a calendar:

  • HPD Annual Registration — due September 1 each year (let this lapse and HPD won't negotiate anything else with you)
  • Bedbug Annual Filing (HPD) — by December 31
  • Window Guard Notices — sent to tenants annually by January 15
  • Stove Knob Cover Notices — annually for buildings with a child under 6
  • Boiler Inspections — annual for low-pressure, triennial for high-pressure

Fair Chance Housing Act

NYC's Fair Chance Housing Act limits how and when landlords may use criminal background checks in tenant screening. Violations can trigger Human Rights Commission complaints with significant penalties. If you screen tenants yourself, update your application and your process now — this is one of the easiest ways for a small Bronx owner to get caught out.

Why This Hits Bronx Owners Hardest

The Bronx has seen aggressive enforcement sweeps over the last two years, especially around lead paint, façade conditions, and lapsed HPD registrations. A single Class C violation at $250–$1,000 per day compounds fast, and the city does not grade on effort.

The owners who stay ahead of it all have one thing in common: a system. A calendar of every deadline above, a vetted vendor list (plumber, façade inspector, energy auditor), and someone reviewing your DOB and HPD violation profiles every single month. Build that yourself, or hand it to a manager who does it for you — but do not leave it to memory. In the Bronx, compliance is the difference between a predictable expense and a five-figure surprise.
