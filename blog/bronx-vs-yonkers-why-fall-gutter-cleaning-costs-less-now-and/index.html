Bronx vs. Yonkers: Why Fall Gutter Cleaning Costs Less Now—and What $150–$400 in Prevention Actually Stops Before Winter Clogs Flood Your Basement

A standard Bronx gutter cleaning runs $150–$300 right now, versus $200–$375 just north in Yonkers — and waiting until the first freeze pushes you into $300–$400 emergency territory with a flooded basement to match.

If you've been staring at the leaves piling up in your gutters and wondering whether it's really worth calling someone in October, the honest answer is yes — and the number is smaller than you think. A standard fall gutter cleaning on a Bronx one-to-four family or small multi-unit (up to about 1,500 linear feet of gutter) runs $150–$300 with a licensed contractor right now. The same job on a similar house in Yonkers typically runs $200–$375, and once we hit November and the first hard freeze, both markets jump to $300–$400 for what becomes an emergency call. That gap — the $50 to $150 you save by being in the Bronx, plus the $150+ you save by not waiting until it ices over — is the whole point of this post.

We manage buildings on both sides of the city line, and every fall we watch the same thing happen: owners who book in mid-October pay the low number, owners who wait until Thanksgiving pay the high number and a plumber, and owners who skip it entirely meet their HPD inspector in February.

Why Is the Bronx Number Lower Than Yonkers Right Now?

Three real reasons, not marketing fluff:

  • Density of vendors. The Bronx has more small gutter and roofing crews per square mile than lower Westchester. Competition keeps the single-family price near $150–$200.
  • Travel and parking. Yonkers jobs around McLean Avenue or Park Hill often mean steeper driveways, longer ladder setups, and the vendor driving out from the Bronx or Mount Vernon anyway. That gets built into the quote.
  • Permit overhead on taller buildings. Once a Yonkers building is three stories or the job turns into any actual gutter repair (not just cleaning), you're closer to a licensed roofer with a permit. In the Bronx, the DOB classifies that as Category B work, and the paperwork is part of the price everywhere — but Yonkers crews tend to pad more for it.

In our experience managing properties from Mosholu Parkway down to Mott Haven, the owners who shop two Bronx vendors in early October almost always land at $175 flat for a row house. Wait six weeks and the same crew quotes $325 because their schedule is full of emergency calls.

What a Clogged Gutter Actually Does to Your Building

Let's talk about the thing itself, because most owners have never been up on a ladder to look. A gutter is just a shallow metal trough bolted to the edge of your roof. Water from the roof runs into it, flows sideways to a downspout (the vertical pipe at the corner), and dumps out at ground level away from the foundation. That's it.

When leaves and silt build up two to four inches deep — which is exactly what happens between late September and late October in the Bronx — the water can't flow sideways anymore. So it does three things, all bad:

  1. Backs up under your roof shingles, soaking the wood underneath.
  2. Spills over the front lip of the gutter and runs down your exterior wall, which is how you get those dark vertical stains below the roofline and bubbling paint on the fascia (the flat board the gutter is nailed to).
  3. Pools at the foundation instead of draining away, and eventually finds its way into the basement.

That third one is where it gets expensive. If you've already got any basement moisture history, this is the moment it turns into a real problem — and the fix pairs naturally with fall basement waterproofing in the $500–$1,500 range. The early warning signs to walk outside and look for this week: visible leaf matter sticking up out of the gutter, dark staining on the siding directly below, and little piles of shingle granules at the bottom of the downspout.

Does a Flooded Basement From Clogged Gutters Count as an HPD Violation?

Yes, and this is the part nobody talks about. Water or dampness in a basement or crawl space is covered under NYC Housing Maintenance Code § 27-2013. If a tenant calls 311 and HPD cites you, that's a Class B violation carrying roughly $500–$1,500 in fines — and it stays on the building record. We've seen Bronx owners who could have paid $175 in October get hit with a $900 HPD fine, a $1,200 basement cleanup, and a tenant rent dispute all from the same clogged downspout. The full picture of what HPD is looking for lives in our Bronx landlord compliance guide.

The quiet benefit of doing the cleaning now: you get a dated vendor receipt and before/after photos. That paper trail is what protects you if a violation ever gets filed — you can show the inspector you did reasonable fall maintenance.

Can You Clean Your Own Gutters, or Do You Need a Licensed Contractor?

Honest answer, because owners ask us this every October:

  • One- or two-family house, gutters under 20 feet off the ground, you're comfortable on a ladder: yes, you can do this yourself. Use a sturdy extension ladder on level ground with a helper spotting you, scoop debris into a bucket by hand (wear gloves — there will be nails and roofing grit in there), then flush with a garden hose to confirm the downspout runs clear. Budget two hours and a sore back.
  • Three stories or more, or you spot any soft/rotted wood on the fascia: stop and call a licensed contractor. This is where DOB Category B rules come in, and rotted fascia means replacement work that triggers a permit. Don't DIY that.
  • You own the building but rent it out and never go up there: pay the $175. The liability if you fall off a ladder at a property you don't live in is not worth the savings.

If you're doing it yourself, the same ladder trip is a great time to glance at the downspout for the symptoms covered in draining and flushing fall leaves from downspouts — a gutter that looks clean up top can still have a packed downspout underneath.

The Mid-October to Halloween Window

Timing matters more than most owners realize. Clean too early — say, Labor Day — and the Bronx's London planes and maples will refill your gutters by late October, and you'll pay for a second cleaning. Clean too late and you're in emergency pricing with ice risk. The sweet spot is roughly October 15 through October 31, after the bulk of leaves have dropped but before the first freeze.

What to Hand the Vendor and Keep for Yourself

Whether you DIY or hire out, keep these for your records:

  • A dated photo of each gutter run before cleaning
  • A dated photo after, showing clear troughs and water flowing from the downspout
  • The vendor's written receipt with date, scope ("cleaned all gutters and downspouts, flushed with water"), and linear footage
  • A one-line note of anything discovered — for example, "fascia soft at northeast corner, roughly 2×3 ft, flagged for spring repair"

Even if you're self-managing, a shared folder on your phone works fine. The point is the paper trail — and the $175 you spent in October instead of the $2,000 you didn't spend in February.
